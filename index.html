Why it exists

Protection should feel like help, not panic.

Phishing works because it creates pressure: pay now, verify now, rescue the parcel now, avoid suspension now. Bye Bye Fishing does not try to become an email security command center. It gives non-technical people a readable warning at the moment they need it.

The extension is intentionally strict around money, passwords, delivery messages, and brand impersonation. A false pause is annoying. A missed fraud attempt can be devastating.

Calm language

No shame, no scary theatre, no blaming the person reading the email.

Local by design

No email upload, no backend, no analytics events, no remote scripts.

Family editable

What it watches

Suspicious claims, domains, links, and pressure.

Brand impersonation

Warns when a message claims a known brand but uses an email address outside the safety list.

Lookalike links

Flags domains with typos, odd shapes, risky endings, shorteners, or public-hosting detours.

Mismatch checks

Compares visible link text with the real destination before trust is assumed.

Pressure language

Looks for urgent payment, delivery, account suspension, credential, and secrecy patterns.

Open source, because trust needs daylight.

The rules should be readable. The extension should be inspectable. Improvements should be reviewable. Bye Bye Fishing is built for a community-maintained safety list, not a black box.

Privacy posture

Boring on purpose.

Bye Bye Fishing reads visible sender information, visible links, and visible message text on supported webmail pages so it can apply local checks. It does not collect or transmit that content. Settings and green-flagged domains stay in browser extension storage.
