Check a message in Outlook webmail

  1. Pay attention to Outlook’s sender indicators

    Outlook can mark an unverified sender with a question mark. This is a reason for caution, not proof that every unverified message is malicious. Hover over the sender to inspect the address.

  2. Confirm an unexpected business request

    A changed invoice or an urgent document invitation can arrive in a convincing conversation. Confirm new payment details through an established contact method, especially when the request asks you to act in secret.

  3. Treat shared-file invitations carefully

    OneDrive and other familiar services can carry user-created content. The hosting service’s name alone does not authenticate the person asking you to sign in, approve access or send money.

  4. Report suspicious email to Microsoft

    Select the message, then choose Report and Report phishing above the reading pane. Reporting does not automatically block future mail from the sender. Follow Microsoft’s current Outlook instructions if your layout differs.

What local warnings can add

Bye Bye Fishing examines the visible sender, subject, message wording and link destinations. It can flag impersonation clues, deceptive addresses and exact URLs on its packaged phishing list, then explain the concern inside webmail. All checks stay on your device.

The developer preview can miss attacks and show false alarms. It does not replace Microsoft’s mail filtering, sender authentication or your organization’s security policies.

Desktop Outlook and managed work accounts

This browser extension targets Outlook on the web. It is not an Outlook desktop add-in and does not scan the native Outlook mobile app. A work or school account may also restrict extension installation; follow your organization’s existing process.

Live Outlook and installed-browser compatibility are still being validated. Chrome and Edge share a preview ZIP; Firefox has a separate temporary-testing package.
