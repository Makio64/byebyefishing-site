Protection rules

Simple rules for not getting rushed into a bad click.

These are the family-facing rules behind the product. They are direct on purpose: pause, verify, then act.

  1. 01

    Do not obey urgency.

    If an email says act now, pay now, or respond within 24 hours, stop and open the real website yourself.

  2. 02

    Read the domain, not the display name.

    "Amazon Support" is just text. The part after @ matters more.

  3. 03

    Never share codes or secrets.

    No real support team should ask for your password, 2FA code, seed phrase, private key, or API key.

  4. 04

    Do money checks twice.

    For invoices, refunds, bank details, fines, tolls, or delivery fees, verify in the official app or by a known phone number.

  5. 05

    Be careful with delivery messages.

    Parcel scams often ask for tiny fees or address confirmation. Open the courier site yourself.

  6. 06

    Short links are a pause signal.

    Links like bit.ly, t.co, or lnkd.in hide where they really go.

  7. 07

    Visible text must match the destination.

    If a link says one domain but opens another, treat it as suspicious until proven safe.

  8. 08

    Personal inboxes are not official billing teams.

    A bank, tax office, payment app, or large brand should not send security requests from a free personal mailbox.

  9. 09

    Typos and odd shapes matter.

    Extra hyphens, random digits, strange endings, very long URLs, and lookalike words are all warning signs.

  10. 10

    Public hosting is not proof of trust.

    Pages on storage buckets or free hosting can be legitimate, but they should not be trusted automatically.

  11. 11

    Ask before embarrassment wins.

    Attackers rely on shame and secrecy. Asking one trusted person is a protection step.

  12. 12

    Green-flag only after checking.

    Only trust a sender domain locally after you have verified it through a known, official channel.

How the extension applies them

One warning can come from several signals.

Known brand, unknown domain

A protected brand appears in the sender or message, but the sender domain is not trusted for that brand.

Lookalike or risky domain

The domain looks like a typo, uses encoded international characters, has suspicious words, or uses a high-risk shape.

Hidden destination

A link is shortened, redirected, unusually long, or the visible domain does not match the real destination.

Pressure content

The email asks for payment, credentials, personal data, delivery confirmation, secrecy, or immediate action.
